Life before HTML & Java: My path to IT

Phase 01: Choosing Careers

I thought I had everything planned out...I was set on becoming a psychologist or maybe pursuing music, although music always felt more like a passion than a career. So it really came down to Law and Psychology: Law because I enjoyed debating for fun and thought I could turn that into a career, and Psychology simply because I found therapists and psychologists interesting, even though I had never really looked deeply into what the careers actually involved. At the same time, most of my friends were choosing Law, so it felt like an easy decision to follow that path and would blend in well. The thing with Law is, it has so many different directions, and I didn’t really understand them, but I felt drawn to criminal law, so I convinced myself that was what I would pursue.

Then IT somehow entered the picture, mostly because I watched way too many movies where people were hacking at insane speeds, and at some point it just clicked in my mind that maybe I wanted to do that too. It wasn’t a deep decision, it was curiosity mixed with imagination, and that’s how IT ended up in my “career options” box. When it came time to apply through CAO, things didn’t go as planned. I wasn’t accepted for Law the way I expected, instead, I got an offer for paralegal studies. I also wasn’t accepted for Psychology, which surprisingly brought a sense of relief because when I looked into it, I realised it would take over 10 years of studying and a PhD, and at the time, I didn’t see myself committing to that.

So I chose ICT(Information and Communication Technology)majoring in application development. Honestly, I had no idea what I was getting myself into. I didn’t realize how many programming languages existed, or that you’re expected to constantly learn and adapt because you never know what you’ll face in the future. That part alone was overwhelming. It made the whole idea of choosing a career feel rushed and uncertain, like we were expected to decide our entire future without really understanding what we were choosing. Looking back, I don’t think I truly thought about my future from grade 10 to grade 12. It only became real when I was forced to choose, when opportunities came to my school and suddenly decisions had to be made. That’s when I realized I didn’t actually know where I was going.

When I got accepted into ICT at CPUT, I felt both relieved and terrified...happy that I had a path, but scared of the unknown. A lot of people around me were shocked by my decision. Some even said I was wasting my time because I hadn’t taken computer-related subjects in high school. At one point, I had even chosen music as a subject because I believed it was my passion and something I could turn into a career, but I was told that music wasn’t something I needed to study formally, that I already had the talent. That just added to the confusion. And as if choosing a career wasn’t overwhelming enough, the reality of actually starting university made things even harder. For the first few months of my first year, I didn’t have accommodation. I was trying to adjust to a completely new environment while also stressing about where I was going to stay. On top of that, NSFAS didn’t fund me in my first year, which added financial pressure to everything else I was already dealing with. It was hard to focus on learning when there were so many uncertainties in the background.

It even got to a point where my family suggested that I should drop out and study closer to home in Durban. And I understood where they were coming from, it made sense from a practical point of view but something in me resisted that idea. I felt like going back home would limit me in a way. I wanted to experience independence even if it was uncomfortable and uncertain. Especially in tech, where opportunities can take you anywhere, I felt like I needed to learn how to adapt, to move and to figure things out on my own. There were so many voices, so many opinions, and so many expectations, and all I could think was: why can’t I just choose something I love? Something that would feel natural to pursue because I actually enjoy it.

But somewhere along the way, even though it wasn’t as immediate or as strong as my interest in Law, I started developing a genuine curiosity for tech. It wasn’t loud or obvious at first but it grew slowly. And eventually, I reached a point where I thought: maybe this is something I can pursue. Maybe there’s nothing wrong with choosing this path, even if it started from uncertainty. If I’m being honest, that entire phase felt like confusion disguised as decision-making, being expected to choose a lifelong career while still figuring yourself out. But looking back now, it wasn’t really about choosing the “perfect” career. It was about choosing something, stepping into the unknown, and learning as you go. And without even realizing it, that’s exactly what I did.
